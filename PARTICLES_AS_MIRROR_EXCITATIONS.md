# Particles as Mirror Excitation Peaks and Troughs

**Particles are not fundamental objects.** They are:
- **Peaks** (compressions, maxima of ψ displacement)
- **Troughs** (expansions, minima of ψ displacement)  
- **Excitation points** where the mirrored compression/expression structure resonates

There is **ONE field**: the scalar displacement ψ on the superfluid lattice.

What we call "particles" are standing patterns, interference peaks, and resonance points in the oscillation cycles of this unified field.

## Peaks and Troughs as Particle Species

### Peaks (Compressions)
- Correspond to matter particles
- Quarks, electrons, etc.
- ψ ≫ 0 locally (field compressed)
- Mass emerges from compression resistance

### Troughs (Expansions)  
- Correspond to antimatter particles
- Antiparticles are mirror-reversed versions
- ψ ≪ 0 locally (field expanded)
- Annihilation occurs when peak meets trough (field cancels)

### Oscillation Rate
The frequency of peak/trough cycling determines the particle's **energy** (E = hν in conventional terms).

In One-Wave: frequency is set by the lattice update rate and the (β, γ) parameters that define how quickly the bounded recurrence rebuilds.

## Hadrons as Multi-Peak Structures

### Proton (three-vortex knot)
- **Three peaks** (compressions) at three spatial positions
- Held together by the Boundary-Tension Weave
- The three peaks oscillate together in phase  
- **Confinement** means extracting one peak requires overcoming the weave cost (Knot Lock)

### Pion (two-vortex knot)
- **One peak + one trough** (quark + antiquark)  
- Smaller boundary, faster oscillation
- Lower mass (lighter binding structure)
- Can annihilate because peak and trough are in opposition
