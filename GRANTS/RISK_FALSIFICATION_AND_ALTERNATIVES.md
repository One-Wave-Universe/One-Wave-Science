# Risk, Falsification, and Alternative Strategies

## Scientific risk

The central One-Wave hypotheses may be wrong.

That possibility is built into the program.

The project remains valuable if it:
- eliminates unsupported formulations;
- produces reusable experimental tooling;
- clarifies which mechanisms are distinguishable;
- establishes quantitative upper bounds;
- documents negative results cleanly.

## Major risks

### Risk 1 — Continuous-lattice model is under-specified
**Mitigation:** require equations, units, boundary conditions, and discriminating predictions before physical interpretation.

### Risk 2 — Cross-domain similarities are only analogy
**Mitigation:** require dimensionless scaling and out-of-domain predictive tests.

### Risk 3 — Hardware effects are ordinary circuit behavior
**Mitigation:** run conventional controls first and use the same instrumentation and analysis.

### Risk 4 — Measurement artifacts dominate
**Mitigation:** calibrate Wave Reader against known sources; characterize noise, drift, bandwidth, common-mode rejection, and reference stability.

### Risk 5 — Simulation success is mistaken for physical validation
**Mitigation:** separate numerical correctness from empirical support in every result table.

### Risk 6 — Associative-memory modules add no advantage
**Mitigation:** compare against simple lookup, nearest-neighbor, conventional Hopfield, and adaptive allocation baselines.

### Risk 7 — Repository breadth overwhelms the proposal
**Mitigation:** fund one narrow experimental spine first; treat the wider architecture as future work.

## Falsification standard

Each funded hypothesis must specify in advance:
- what observation would count against it;
- what comparator is used;
- what parameter range is tested;
- what uncertainty is tolerated;
- what result causes the claim to be revised or dismissed.

## Alternative strategy principle

If a novel mechanism fails but the measurement or software platform is sound, pivot the next aim toward the best-supported mechanism rather than altering the acceptance rule after seeing the data.
