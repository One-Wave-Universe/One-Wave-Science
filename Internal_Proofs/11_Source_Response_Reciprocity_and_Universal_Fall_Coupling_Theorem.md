# 11 — Source–Response Reciprocity and Universal-Fall Coupling Theorem

## Purpose

Close the hidden coupling assumption exposed by Proof 10 as far as logic alone can close it.

Proof 09 derives a candidate field shape. Proof 10 then assumes one common mass/source coupling in order to obtain reciprocal conservative three-body dynamics.

A-115 already writes a source term `J_source` and asks the exterior solution to contain the measured Mass Effect `M_eff`, while C-318 independently defines inertial Mass Effect from the carried-pattern energy response. The repository does **not** yet derive why those two quantities must be proportional.

This proof separates them and asks:

> Within a linear source/response field model, what coupling form is required if pair momentum is conserved and freely falling test bodies respond independently of composition?

The result is algebraically exact.

---

## 1. Keep the three body properties separate

For each bounded body `i`, define:

- `m_i` — inertial Mass Effect, as targeted by C-318;
- `q_i` — source charge entering the candidate field source `J_source`;
- `beta_i` — response charge converting a field gradient into force on the body.

Do **not** set any two equal by definition.

Let the field generated by body `j` be linear in its source charge:

\[
\psi_j(\mathbf x)=q_j\,G(\mathbf x-\mathbf r_j),
\]

where `G` may be the Proof-09 gradient–curvature Green function or any other common translation-invariant scalar Green function.

Let the force on body `i` due to `j` be

\[
\mathbf F_{i\leftarrow j}
=-\beta_i\nabla\psi_j(\mathbf r_i).
\]

Write the common antisymmetric geometric kernel as

\[
\mathbf K_{ij}
=-\nabla G(\mathbf r_i-\mathbf r_j),
\qquad
\mathbf K_{ji}=-\mathbf K_{ij}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf F_{i\leftarrow j}
=\beta_i q_j\mathbf K_{ij}.
}
\]

The reverse force is

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf F_{j\leftarrow i}
=-\beta_j q_i\mathbf K_{ij}.
}
\]

---

## 2. Pair reciprocity / momentum conservation

For an isolated pair to have zero net internal force,

\[
\mathbf F_{i\leftarrow j}
+
\mathbf F_{j\leftarrow i}
=0.
\]

For nonzero `K_ij`, this requires

\[
\boxed{
\beta_i q_j=\beta_j q_i
}
\]

for every pair `i,j`.

Assume the source and response charges in the gravity branch are nonzero. Rearranging gives

\[
\frac{\beta_i}{q_i}
=
\frac{\beta_j}{q_j}
\]

for every pair.

Therefore there exists one common constant `c` such that

\[
\boxed{
\beta_i=c\,q_i.
}
\]

### Result A

**Pair reciprocity alone forces source and response charge to be proportional to each other.**

It does **not** yet force either one to track inertial mass.

---

## 3. Composition-independent free fall

Place several different test bodies in the same externally generated field `psi_ext`.

The force on test body `i` is

\[
\mathbf F_i
=-\beta_i\nabla\psi_{\rm ext}.
\]

Its inertial acceleration is

\[
\mathbf a_i
=
\frac{\mathbf F_i}{m_i}
=
-\frac{\beta_i}{m_i}\nabla\psi_{\rm ext}.
\]

For the acceleration to be independent of the test body's composition or identity under this model,

\[
\frac{\beta_i}{m_i}
=
\frac{\beta_j}{m_j}
\]

for all test bodies.

Therefore there exists one common constant `d` such that

\[
\boxed{
\beta_i=d\,m_i.
}
\]

### Result B

**Composition-independent free fall forces response charge to be proportional to inertial Mass Effect.**

This is a weak/Newtonian test-body statement. It is not the full relativistic equivalence principle.

---

## 4. Combine the two requirements

From pair reciprocity,

\[
\beta_i=cq_i.
\]

From universal fall,

\[
\beta_i=dm_i.
\]

Therefore

\[
q_i=\frac{d}{c}m_i.
\]

Define

\[
c_\beta=d,
\qquad
c_q=\frac dc.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\beta_i=c_\beta m_i,
\qquad
q_i=c_qm_i.
}
\]

### Coupling theorem

Within the declared linear source/response architecture:

\[
\boxed{
\text{pair reciprocity + composition-independent free fall}
\Longrightarrow
q\propto m_{\rm eff}
\text{ and }
\beta\propto m_{\rm eff}.
}
\]

This is the precise condition that Proof 10 had previously inserted as a common mass/source assumption.

---

## 5. Reduction to one universal pair constant

Substitute

\[
q_j=c_qm_j,
\qquad
\beta_i=c_\beta m_i.
\]

Then

\[
\mathbf F_{i\leftarrow j}
=
(c_\beta c_q)m_im_j\mathbf K_{ij}.
\]

Divide by inertial mass `m_i`:

\[
\mathbf a_{i\leftarrow j}
=
(c_\beta c_q)m_j\mathbf K_{ij}.
\]

Define

\[
K=c_\beta c_q.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf a_{i\leftarrow j}=K m_j\mathbf K_{ij}.
}
\]

This is exactly the common-coupling structure used by Proof 10.

The test body's own inertial Mass Effect cancels from its acceleration. The source body's Mass Effect remains as the source weight.

## 7. What each requirement does by itself

The two conditions are independent.

### Reciprocity without universal fall

Choose

\[
\beta_i=cq_i
\]

for every body, but let `q_i/m_i` vary.

Then pair forces remain equal and opposite, but

\[
\mathbf a_i\propto\frac{q_i}{m_i}
\]

so different test bodies fall differently.

### Universal fall without reciprocity

Choose

\[
\beta_i=dm_i
\]

but let `q_i/m_i` vary.

Then all test bodies respond to one external field with the same acceleration factor, but a pair has

\[
\beta_iq_j\ne\beta_jq_i
\]

in general, producing a nonzero net internal force.

Therefore neither condition can substitute for the other.

## 9. What remains physically open

This theorem does **not** explain why the underlying recurrence has these couplings. It says what they must look like if the declared macroscopic requirements hold.

Still required:

1. derive `q_i` from the actual four-interaction source deformation of Ground;
2. derive `beta_i` from the actual work required for that recurrence to respond to a Ground slope;
3. show both derivations yield the same `m_eff` proportionality without tuning each body;
4. derive the constants `c_q` and `c_beta` or their product `K` from independently fixed microscopic parameters;
5. test composition dependence at the required precision;
6. test whether nonlinear source superposition changes the theorem outside the linear regime.

The theorem is therefore a **constraint on the missing microscopic bridge**, not a substitute for it.
