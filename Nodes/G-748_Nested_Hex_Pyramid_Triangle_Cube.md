## Hexagon as six pyramids

A regular hexagon plus its center is six equilateral triangles.

Raise a common apex above the center and you get **six triangular pyramids** (tets if the apex height is chosen so lateral edges match; otherwise triangular pyramids with a shared axis).

Dual expression, already named in conversation:

- tip-to-tip (two apices, hexagonal bipyramid)
- base-to-base (two hexagonal faces kissing through a mid-plane)

Hexagonal bipyramid census (regular):

\[
V=8,\quad E=18,\quad F=12,\quad V-E+F=2.
\]

Eighteen edges are twelve lateral plus six equator. The six equatorial triangles are the hex split. Six pyramids, six triplets, two sets of three axis pairs — same 3:1 / 6:1 distinction D-411 already owns. Do not call that 3:1 the neural aggregation ratio.

The circumsphere through the eight vertices is the first spherical shell of this nest. It is a bounding sphere of a polyhedron, not proof that "the octahedron is the sphere."

## Triangle containing cube and hex

Midpoint subdivision of a triangle makes four congruent triangles: three corners plus one inverted middle.

In 3D the same midpoint move on a tetrahedron yields four corner tets plus a central octahedron. The octahedron's equatorial section is a hexagon. A cube can sit with its space diagonal along an octa/tet axis so that the hex silhouette and the cube share the same mid-plane. That is a **projection / section coincidence**, not "a triangle is a cube."

Receipt to keep:

- 1 triangle → 4 small triangles (2D)
- 1 tet → 4 corner tets + 1 octa (3D)
- octa mid-belt → hexagon
- cube section / silhouette may share that hex

Midpoints of one scale are candidate **points** of the next scale. That is PPF nesting talk, still Yellow until C2–C4 rotations exist.
