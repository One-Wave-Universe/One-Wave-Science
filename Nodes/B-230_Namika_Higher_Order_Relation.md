# B-230 — Namika Higher-Order Relation

## Purpose

Define the relation formed when two complete six-operation systems couple at a higher level without inventing an internal Gate 7.

## No Internal Gate 7

A complete local system retains six internal operations. Coupling two complete systems does not append a seventh serial gate to either system.

Let complete systems `A` and `B` expose resolved relational outputs

`R_A = (D_A, P_A, S_A, R0_A)`

`R_B = (D_B, P_B, S_B, R0_B)`

for Direction, Phase, Strength, and Reference.

The higher-order relation is named **Namika**:

`N_AB = Namika(R_A, R_B, R_shared)`.

`Namika` is therefore an inter-system relation operator, not `Gate_7`.

## Structural Requirement

Before two systems can form a valid Namika relation, their exposed references must be compatible or explicitly transformed into one shared reference:

`Compat(R0_A, R0_B, R_shared) = true`.

The resulting higher-order relation must preserve the same external type expected by recursive scaling:

`Type(N_AB) = Type(R_A) = Type(R_B)`

unless a future node explicitly derives a different inter-system contract.

## Open Operator

The exact Namika operator is not yet fixed. Candidate ingredients may include:

- shared differential,
- tensor-like combined relation,
- phase alignment/misalignment,
- strength balance,
- reference reconciliation,
- Step-6 Continue/Hold/Break-Reroute evaluation.

No candidate is promoted to the final operator in this node.

## Failure Conditions

The concept fails its architectural purpose if:

- Namika is treated as a seventh internal gate,
- coupling requires rewriting the six-operation kernel,
- the coupled system loses reference compatibility,
- or every higher-level coupling requires a unique new grammar.
