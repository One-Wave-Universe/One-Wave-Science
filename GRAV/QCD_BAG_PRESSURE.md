# QCD bag pressure

MIT bag: quarks free *inside* a region. Outside, they are forbidden. All of the strong force is dumped into a constant vacuum pressure B.

    p = p_quarks+gluons - B
    ε = ε_quarks+gluons + B

B is energy density of the false-empty inside relative to the true vacuum outside. Same number is a pressure: the outside vacuum squeezes the bag.

## Numbers people actually use

| | B | B^{1/4} |
|---|---|---|
| original MIT hadron fit | 57.5 MeV/fm³ | 145 MeV |
| to push Tc toward ~170 MeV in a naive bag | ~400 MeV/fm³ | ~235 MeV |
| strange-matter stability window (typical) | | ~148–159 MeV |
| Hagedorn-bag crossover fits | | ~250 MeV |

B is not a constant of nature. It is a knob that sets the scale of the skin's squeeze.

Equilibrium of a static spherical bag (no extra surface term): 2T/R ~ B × volume balance with quark kinetic pressure. Add surface tension γ and you get a Young–Laplace piece 2γ/R on the wall — bulk B plus skin γ, same story as last file.

Deconfine when p_inside beats B (and γ). Then the bag is no longer cheaper than free color. Pressure of the confined phase can even read negative in simple bookkeeping — vacuum winning.

One-wave read: B is κ of the color fluid at hadron scale. Tear / hole is that squeeze at gravity's κ. Different B (different fit) is already why “one bag size” never existed.
