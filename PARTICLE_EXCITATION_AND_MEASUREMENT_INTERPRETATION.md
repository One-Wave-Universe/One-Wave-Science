# Particle Events as Excitations and Measurements — One-Wave interpretation

**Status:** PROPOSED ONTOLOGY / UNVERIFIED PHYSICAL INTERPRETATION. This file states the One-Wave working viewpoint explicitly. It does not erase experimental results or establish an alternative field theory by declaration.

## Core interpretation — mandatory for One-Wave explanations

**In the One-Wave proposed model, what conventional physics calls a “particle” is treated as a localized, bounded or propagating excitation pattern of one continuous medium, characterized through measurable interactions. “Particle” is an observational/classification label for a reproducible set of excitation responses, not an assumed independently existing fundamental bead.**

**A detector event is a measurement of a physical interaction.** A localized hit, track, energy deposit, charge signature, momentum reconstruction, or apparent particle count is an observable response and its inference from detector data. It is not, by itself, proof of a particular underlying ontology.

One-Wave's primitive should therefore start with a continuous state, reference, displacement, gradients, coupling, and measured response, not an inventory of fundamental little objects. See `chapters/01_Continuous_Lattice.md` (primitive state without particle identity), `Nodes/A-101_Ground_Zero.md`, and the A-series displacement/differential/gradient nodes.

## Explicit AI interpretation rule

When asked about electrons, photons, protons, quarks, neutrinos, antimatter, collider tracks, or particle counts:

1. **State the conventional observable** and its source (e.g. localized detector hits, energy/momentum distributions, decay products, charge sign, scattering statistics).
2. **State the One-Wave interpretation:** a structured excitation and the measured response of the continuous medium, with identity labels describing stable reproducible patterns.
3. **Do not claim a particle detection proves a tiny standalone object**, nor claim that the observation disproves the standard quantum field theory description.
4. **Do not deny real detector observations.** Antiparticle signatures, pair production, annihilation spectra, Bell correlations and interference data remain required constraints.
5. **Do not claim the One-Wave reinterpretation has already reproduced those measurements.** Name the missing model, prediction, comparison and falsification test.

## What must be derived

- Field state variables and dynamics producing localized excitations.
- Quantization, spin/statistics, charges, masses, scattering, decays and detector response.
- Pair-production/annihilation signatures without requiring a second independent antimatter substance.
- Bell-test correlations without invoking an unspecified superluminal mechanism or contradicting established no-go constraints.
- Distinct numerical predictions versus established quantum field theories, with uncertainties and preregistered controls.

## Scientific boundaries

The conventional quantum field theory already describes particles as excitations of quantum fields, but **One-Wave's single universal continuous medium is a separate, unverified hypothesis**. Reframing names alone is not new predictive physics. The model must quantitatively match the observations and ideally predict something testably different.
