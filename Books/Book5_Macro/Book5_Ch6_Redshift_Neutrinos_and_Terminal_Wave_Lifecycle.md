## 1. Observation Before Interpretation
Redshift is a measured change in spectral wavelength. Neutrino detectors register low-coupling interaction events with measurable energy, direction, timing, and flavor likelihood. Stars are observed sources of neutrino populations. These are the observation layer.

Expanding space, particle ontology, terminal flattening, and cosmic return are interpretations. The repository must never merge these layers merely because a label became popular enough to acquire office furniture.

## 2. Non-Expanding One-Wave Position
One-Wave contains no age or expansion of the universe. Distance is propagation path length. Galaxies remain within one persistent field. The Milky Way-Andromeda approach therefore requires no rule that spatial expansion operates globally but politely excuses itself from a bound group.

E-528 models redshift through static transport. E-531 extends that law into an optical lifecycle containing amplitude, wavelength, coherence, curvature, and coupling.

## 3. The Optical Lifecycle
A light wave is electromagnetically accessible while its residual oscillatory contrast and coupling exceed the relevant boundary threshold. With propagation, wavelength can lengthen while amplitude, curvature, or coherence change. The optical range is the path length to the terminal coupling boundary, not the age of a universe.

The furthest current detections give a lower bound on that range. They do not identify wave death unless a physical ceiling and transition are measured.

## 4. Interference and Coherence
Waves from stars and galaxies overlap. Stable phase relationships reinforce; unstable components average or cancel. A galaxy is treated as a bounded recurrent network whose shared rotation, plasma, magnetic structure, and repeated frequency families may preserve a structured far-field envelope.

The claim is not established by a bright telescope image. It must be tested using polarization, linewidth, correlation, interferometric visibility, timing, and population statistics.

E-532 predicts terminal convergence: after source class and detector response are controlled, the most propagated low-coupling population should become more uniform in a normalized terminal-state coordinate.

## 5. Neutrinos as Near-Flat Lifecycle States
E-533 interprets neutrino measurements as late, low-coupling configurations of a wave becoming flatter relative to the field baseline. Flatter means smaller residual amplitude, shallower curvature, and weaker boundary coupling. It does not mean lower measured energy automatically equals older wave.

Flavor states are not assigned a convenient death order. Their lifecycle mapping must be derived from measured oscillation probabilities, energy, baseline, source, and interaction channel.

## 6. Stars Produce Neutrinos
Stars demonstrably generate source-linked neutrino populations. One-Wave interprets stellar pressure, fusion-linked transitions, recurrent boundaries, and collapse as short routes into the low-coupling state space. Long-distance optical propagation is another proposed route.

Thus the lifecycle contains multiple entry paths:

```text
stellar boundary event -> low-coupling state
optical propagation -> redshift boundary -> low-coupling state
```

The populations may share a state space without being identical at entry.

## 7. Peak-to-Trough Research Program
E-535 registers a comparison between optical lifecycle observables and neutrino oscillation envelopes. It compares normalized data vectors, not cartoon crests from unrelated measurements.

## 8. Andromeda Test Case
In One-Wave, the Milky Way and Andromeda move in one non-expanding field. Their approach is ordinary system motion. Redshift elsewhere must therefore be explained by propagation and source dynamics, not used as an automatic ruler for expanding distance.

This case does not by itself derive the static redshift law. It removes the need for a local exception and places the burden where it belongs: on the transport equations.
