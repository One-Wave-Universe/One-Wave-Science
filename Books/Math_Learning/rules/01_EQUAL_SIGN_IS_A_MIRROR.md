# Rule 1 — The Equal Sign Is a Mirror

The equal sign is not a signal that says "the answer comes next."

It is a mirror between two expressions.

Whatever is on the left and whatever is on the right must reflect the same value.

They do not have to look the same.

Example:

`3 + 2 = 5`

The left side looks different from the right side, but both are the same value.

Think of it as:

**left value | = | right value**

The equal sign is the mirror line.

If one side changes, the reflected relationship has to stay true.

That is why later, when solving equations, you cannot just change one side and ignore the other. The mirror has to keep matching in value.

## What this rule means

- `=` means both sides have the same value.
- The two sides can be written differently.
- The equal sign connects two matching values.
- If an operation changes the relationship, the mirror is broken.

## Worked examples

`4 = 4`

Same value on both sides.

`2 + 3 = 5`

Different writing, same value.

`6 - 1 = 2 + 3`

Both sides are 5, so the mirror holds.

## Not equal

`2 + 3 = 6`

Left side is 5. Right side is 6.

The mirror does not match, so the equation is false.

## Why this comes first

Almost all early algebra depends on understanding this before any balance or undoing rules make sense.

If the learner sees `=` as "answer comes next," later equation rules feel arbitrary.

If the learner sees `=` as a mirror between equal values, later moves have a reason.

## Practice structure

For this rule:

- 3 problems build the mirror idea.
- 3 problems lock it down with different-looking expressions.
- Live practice problems do not show the answer.
- Every problem links back to this rule.
