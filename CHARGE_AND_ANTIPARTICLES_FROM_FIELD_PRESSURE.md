## 1. How Charge Emerges from Field Topology

### The Field Configuration

Consider a bounded knot holding a compression peak:

```
        ψ(r)
          ↑
          │     ╱╲
        0 ├───╱  ╲────────
          │╱      ╲
         -│        ╲    
          │        peak
          │  (electron)
          └─────r────→
```

Inside the bounded region Ω:
- ψ is compressed (ψ ≠ 0)
- ∇ψ points outward from the peak (pressure gradient)
- At the boundary ∂Ω, the field falls off sharply (surface tension)

### Pressure as Charge

**Electric field pressure** in the One-Wave picture:

The gradient ∇ψ creates a **confinement pressure** that holds the peak bounded. This pressure is what we measure as **electric charge**.

More precisely:
- The charge of an electron (−e) measures the **inward gradient pressure** that creates and holds the compression peak
- The charge of a positron (+e) measures the **outward gradient pressure** that opposes peak formation

**Same magnitude, opposite direction.**

This is why:
- Electrons and positrons have equal mass (same bounded knot structure)
- They have opposite charges (opposite gradient pressures)
- They can coexist in a symmetric configuration until one is extracted

### The Boundary-Tension Weave Role

From C-317, the Boundary-Tension Weave provides:
1. **Surface energy** σ_T (confines the peak)
2. **Phase-locking** κ_T (keeps internal structure stable)  
3. **Twist energy** η_T (angular momentum)

The **charge emerges from the gradient of these pressure terms**.

For a spherical bounded knot:
- Pressure inside the knot: P_inside = σ_T / R (surface tension contribution)
- Pressure outside: P_outside = 0
- **Net pressure difference** = charge magnitude

## 2. Pair Production: How Positrons Are Created

### Energy Threshold for Pair Creation

When a high-energy photon (E > 2m_e c²) interacts with the lattice, it can create an electron-positron pair.

**In One-Wave terms:**

A photon is a **transverse oscillation** of the lattice (B-like mode from D-602). When its energy exceeds the threshold to create a new bounded knot:

1. The photon energy deforms the lattice locally
2. Two new bounded regions form (called a "neck split" in C-317)
3. One region holds a compression peak (electron)
4. The other holds an expansion trough (positron)

The **threshold energy is 2m_e c²** because:
- Creating one bounded knot requires energy ≈ m_e c² (from the four-interaction hold + surface energy)
- Creating two requires ≈ 2m_e c²
- The positron region starts as an expansion trough (lower energy than a second compression peak would require)

### Why Positrons Form in Pairs

The lattice prefers **symmetric configurations**:
- A compression peak alone breaks symmetry (creates a stress gradient)
- A compression peak + expansion trough form a balanced dipole
- The dipole minimizes energy cost to the surrounding lattice

The positron (as expansion trough) is energetically favorable because:
- It reduces the total gradient stress
- It's confined by the same boundary structure that confines the electron
- The two phases lock together (phase-locking energy κ_T)

## 3. Annihilation: Phase Cancellation

### When Electron Meets Positron

When an electron (compression peak, ψ = +A) encounters a positron (expansion trough, ψ = −A):

**Energy release:**

This appears as **gamma rays** (high-energy photon emission).

The gamma rays are **transverse oscillations** that propagate freely because they don't require a bounded knot structure—they're just waves on the lattice.

## 4. Charge Separation and Dipole Moments

### Why Electrons Have Negative Charge

Electrons are **compression peaks** that form first in the early universe (or in pair production).

The compression peak creates an **inward-pointing gradient**:
- ∇ψ points radially inward (toward the peak)
- This gradient is defined as **negative charge** by convention

When we measure the electron's electric field:
- It points **outward** from the electron (repulsive)
- But this is the **potential gradient**, not the knot pressure
- The internal gradient (negative) creates the external field (negative charge)

### Why Positrons Have Positive Charge

Positrons form as **expansion troughs** paired with electrons.

The expansion trough creates an **outward-pointing gradient**:
- ∇ψ points radially outward (away from the trough)
- This gradient is defined as **positive charge**

### Hadron Charges Emerge From Quark Phases

For a proton (three-vortex knot with u, u, d quarks):

**Quark charges:**
- Up quark: compression (−1/3 × 3) = −1 internal, appears as +2/3 external
- Down quark: compression (−1/3 × 2) = −2/3 internal, appears as +1/3 external

The **color charge** (red/green/blue) is a **phase offset** of the vortex phases in the three-vortex knot.

Three vortex phases at 120° phase separation (red, green, blue) create a **color-neutral** bound state—the white proton.
