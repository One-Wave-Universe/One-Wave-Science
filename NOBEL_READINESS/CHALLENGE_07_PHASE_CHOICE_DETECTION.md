## Detector boundary

Ordinary square-law optical detectors respond approximately to intensity,

[
Ipropto |E|^2,
]

so equal positive and negative electric-field amplitudes produce the same instantaneous intensity.

Therefore a standard photodiode does **not** directly distinguish a crest from a trough.

To test field sign / phase, the experiment requires a coherent reference, for example:
- interferometry;
- homodyne detection;
- heterodyne detection;
- another phase-sensitive reference interaction.

This distinction is mandatory. Any claim that an ordinary intensity detector directly reads positive-vs-negative field sign is rejected.

## Point / Path / Field mapping

Candidate One-Wave mapping:

- **Point** — local oscillator phase/state;
- **Path** — accumulated propagation phase;
- **Field** — interaction/reference environment at readout.

This mapping is a representation until it produces a distinct quantitative prediction.

## Key scientific question

Does the "choice at interaction" formulation reproduce all standard interference and phase-sensitive measurement statistics exactly?

If yes, it is an L3-compatible interpretation.

To become new physics, One-Wave must predict a measurable departure tied to one or more independently controlled variables such as:
- path length;
- propagation time;
- source phase;
- detector/reference phase;
- frequency;
- coherence time;
- detector response window.

## Strong discriminator

Predeclare a phase-sensitive experiment in which standard quantum optics and the One-Wave interaction rule produce different quantitative outcome probabilities or correlations.

No novelty is claimed if the two formulations remain mathematically equivalent for all measured observables.

## Failure condition

The hypothesis fails as a distinct physical theory if:
- it requires an ordinary intensity detector to distinguish field sign;
- it predicts a phase dependence already completely accounted for by standard interference;
- or no measurable observable differs from standard quantum-optical predictions.
