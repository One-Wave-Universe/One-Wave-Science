# Challenge 04 — Solar Coronal Heating Boundary

**Target:** L2 energy-flow derivation -> L3 recovery of known coronal constraints -> L4 quantitative heating prediction.

## Standard problem

The solar corona reaches roughly (10^6) K while the visible surface is around (6	imes10^3) K. Current solar physics considers magnetic reconnection, Alfvén-wave/turbulent dissipation, wave-particle interactions, and related mechanisms. The unresolved question is how the required energy is transported, converted, partitioned, and maintained across the transition region and corona.

## One-Wave question

Can the proposed boundary / shell / Point-Path-Field picture derive a quantitative upward energy flux and dissipation law that reproduces the observed temperature rise **without introducing a free heating function fitted to the corona**?

Then derive, rather than assume:
- how magnetic/rotational energy crosses the photosphere/chromosphere boundary;
- where the conversion rate peaks;
- why heating persists with altitude;
- the predicted partition between electrons, protons, and heavier ions if the model addresses species;
- the relation between heating and solar-wind acceleration.

## Required observational outputs

A useful One-Wave model must predict at least:
- radial temperature profile (T(r));
- heating power per unit area or volume;
- dependence on magnetic topology/open vs closed field;
- a signature associated with switchbacks/reconnection/waves;
- solar-wind speed or energy flux as a coupled output.

## Strong discriminator

Predeclare one measurable radial or spectral signature for which One-Wave differs from a standard reconnection/turbulent-heating model.

## Failure condition

If the model merely says "boundary tension releases energy" but cannot close the measured energy budget or locate the deposition quantitatively, it remains analogy, not a coronal-heating solution.
