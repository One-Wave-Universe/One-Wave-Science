# Nobel-Readiness Science Dossier

## Purpose

Prepare One-Wave Science for rigorous external physics review and possible future nomination support.

This is not a Nobel submission. The practical target is a compact dossier that lets an independent physicist evaluate the work without reconstructing the whole repository.

## Three proof classes

### A. Logical / mathematical proof

These claims can be established from declared definitions, axioms, transformations, conservation laws, symmetries, and derivations.

A result in this class must include:
- definitions;
- assumptions;
- derivation;
- dimensional consistency where physical quantities are used;
- boundary conditions;
- proof that the conclusion follows from the premises;
- explicit statement of whether the result is a theorem of the One-Wave formalism only or also reproduces a known physical law.

No hardware evidence is required to prove an internal mathematical result.

### B. Recovery / equivalence proof

These claims show that the One-Wave formalism reproduces an already-established result of physics or mathematics.

Required:
- standard target equation/result;
- One-Wave starting assumptions;
- line-by-line mapping;
- limiting assumptions;
- numerical or symbolic equality within stated tolerance;
- identification of any extra free parameter.

This is stronger than analogy. A verbal resemblance does not count.

### C. Empirical physical claim

These claims assert that nature uses a proposed One-Wave mechanism or that the theory predicts a measurable effect beyond established models.

Required:
- observable;
- control or competing model;
- quantitative prediction;
- uncertainty;
- falsification condition;
- independent replication where practical.

Mathematics can prove the prediction follows from the model. Observation is still required to establish that the model describes nature.

5. `FALSIFICATION.md` — conditions that would kill each claim.
6. `EXTERNAL_REVIEW_PACKET.md` — concise material for an outside physicist.

## Rule

Do not inflate an empirical hypothesis into a proof, and do not demote a genuine mathematical proof merely because it has not yet been physically tested.

The dossier should always say which kind of proof is being claimed.
