# Rule 1 — Balanced Change

If two sides are equal, you can do the same operation to the whole left side and the whole right side, and they stay equal.

`=` is a same-value claim. This rule is why algebra moves are allowed at all.

The thing to imprint: **I am preserving the relationship by changing both sides equally.**

Do **not** teach “move the 3 across and change its sign” yet. That shortcut comes later, if at all. No coefficients. No matched-pair cancellation language yet.

## Lesson shape

3 build-up (worked path shown) then 3 lock-down (learner writes the next line).

### Build-up 1 — `R1-BU-1`

Start: `3 + 2 = 5`

Add 4 to both whole sides:

`3 + 2 + 4 = 5 + 4`

Now: `9 = 9`

### Build-up 2 — `R1-BU-2`

Start: `10 = 7 + 3`

Subtract 2 from both whole sides:

`10 - 2 = 7 + 3 - 2`

Now: `8 = 8`

### Build-up 3 — `R1-BU-3`

Start: `2 + 3 = 6 - 1`

Add 1 to both whole sides:

`2 + 3 + 1 = 6 - 1 + 1`

Now: `6 = 6`

### Lock-down 1 — `R1-LD-1`

Start: `x + 3 = 8`

Learner writes: `x + 3 - 3 = 8 - 3`

Do not put `x = 5` on the live card.

### Lock-down 2 — `R1-LD-2`

Start: `x - 4 = 6`

Learner writes: `x - 4 + 4 = 6 + 4`

### Lock-down 3 — `R1-LD-3`

Start: `5 = 5`

Broken line: `5 + 2 = 5`

Learner says the relationship does **not** still hold, because only one side changed.
