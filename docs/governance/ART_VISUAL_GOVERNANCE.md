# Art / Visual Governance

This file governs selection of art, illustration, story imagery, chapter visuals, Miniverse / Mega City aesthetics, and other non-measurement visual presentation across the repository.

## 1. Technical truth comes first

Technical diagrams, plots, scope traces, geometry figures, circuit layouts, and simulations must match their authoritative source data, Nodes, equations, architecture, or measured result.

Aesthetic preference may choose among technically correct presentations. It may not change the underlying data or make a hypothesis look established.

## 2. Art may be submitted competitively

Multiple humans or AIs may submit alternate visual treatments for the same scene, chapter, city district, character, architecture view, or story concept.

Each submission should identify:

- contributor / AI name or identifier;
- date;
- branch/PR or asset path;
- intended use;
- source brief / chapter / Node / scene;
- whether it is technical, conceptual, illustrative, mythological, or purely artistic;
- any elements that are deliberately symbolic rather than literal.

Alternative versions should remain separate until selection.

## 3. Voting and ranking

Contributors may vote or rank visual alternatives for:

- clarity;
- emotional impact;
- readability;
- consistency with the surrounding book/world;
- faithfulness to the brief;
- usefulness for teaching;
- visual quality.

Votes are advisory evidence for selection, not automatic merge authority.

## 4. Human visual veto

Until AI collaborators can reliably inspect the final rendered image/artwork in the same form the human viewer sees it, the project owner retains final veto authority over art and aesthetic selection.

That veto applies to:

- character appearance;
- mythology/story art;
- book and chapter illustrations;
- Miniverse / Mega City look and atmosphere;
- decorative diagrams;
- visual identity / branding;
- scene composition;
- cover art;
- any visual where appearance is itself part of the quality decision.

The veto may reject an otherwise popular or technically valid visual without rejecting the underlying technical work.

## 5. AI visibility rule

An AI should not claim that an image "looks right," is aesthetically superior, or matches a visual brief unless that AI actually had access to the rendered result through a tool or workflow that lets it inspect the image.

If an AI can only inspect source code, SVG/XML, prompts, filenames, metadata, or text descriptions, it must say that it is evaluating structure/specification rather than the final appearance.

## 6. Technical visuals versus art

For technical visuals:

- correctness is mandatory;
- human veto still applies to presentation quality;
- rejected presentation may be revised without changing the technical result;
- axes, labels, units, legends, and claim boundaries must remain accurate.

For artistic/story visuals:

- aesthetic voting is encouraged;
- multiple styles may coexist in proposals;
- final selection remains subject to human veto;
- symbolism must not be confused with engineering or scientific evidence.

## 7. Merge rule

A visual/art branch is ready for merge only when:

1. the contributor is identified;
2. the intended use is documented;
3. technical-source links are present when applicable;
4. overlapping visual alternatives have been compared;
5. the project owner has not vetoed the selected art;
6. if multiple AIs contributed, the normal merge-agreement rules are satisfied;
7. the final asset is stored with enough metadata that another contributor can understand its origin and purpose.

## 8. Foreman duty

The construction foreman keeps visual work orderly by:

- assigning separate visual branches when multiple styles are being explored;
- keeping technical diagrams tied to source truth;
- keeping art and mythology clearly labeled;
- preventing aesthetic disagreements from overwriting valid technical work;
- presenting competing visual options for review instead of silently choosing one;
- recording the selected version and any vetoed/deferred alternatives.
