## 1. The slogan to audit

A common summary of general relativity is:

> Matter tells spacetime how to curve; curved spacetime tells matter how to move.

This is useful shorthand for coupled equations. It becomes misleading when heard as if a drawn rubber sheet or a shape on paper physically reaches out and pushes an object.

The diagrams are representations. The theory's actual content is mathematical relations among the metric, curvature, stress-energy, proper time, and trajectories.

## 2. Accepted field equation

General relativity relates spacetime curvature to stress-energy through Einstein's field equation

$$
G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}
=
\frac{8\pi G}{c^4}T_{\mu\nu},
$$

where

- $g_{\mu\nu}$ is the metric,
- $G_{\mu\nu}$ is the Einstein tensor constructed from spacetime curvature,
- $T_{\mu\nu}$ is the stress-energy tensor,
- $\Lambda$ is the cosmological constant,
- $G$ is Newton's gravitational constant.

The Einstein tensor is

$$
G_{\mu\nu}=R_{\mu\nu}-\frac12 Rg_{\mu\nu}.
$$

These are not pictures. They are tensor equations whose predictions can be compared with clocks, light propagation, orbital motion, lensing, and gravitational-wave observations.

## 3. Motion of a freely falling test body

A freely falling ideal test body follows a geodesic satisfying

$$
\frac{d^2x^\mu}{d\tau^2}
+
\Gamma^\mu_{\alpha\beta}
\frac{dx^\alpha}{d\tau}
\frac{dx^\beta}{d\tau}
=0,
$$

where $\tau$ is proper time and $\Gamma^\mu_{\alpha\beta}$ are Christoffel symbols derived from the metric.

The Christoffel symbols are

$$
\Gamma^\mu_{\alpha\beta}
=
\frac12 g^{\mu\nu}
\left(
\partial_\alpha g_{\nu\beta}
+
\partial_\beta g_{\nu\alpha}
-
\partial_\nu g_{\alpha\beta}
\right).
$$

This is the precise mathematical content behind the phrase "geometry tells matter how to move."

## 4. Coordinate picture versus invariant physics

The components $g_{\mu\nu}$ and $\Gamma^\mu_{\alpha\beta}$ depend on the coordinate description.

Physical predictions must ultimately be expressed through invariant or operationally meaningful quantities such as

- proper time along a worldline,
- frequency ratios between clocks,
- invariant curvature scalars where useful,
- geodesic deviation,
- observable angular deflection,
- orbital precession,
- gravitational-wave strain measured by detectors.

A change of coordinates can make the mathematical picture look very different without changing the underlying observable prediction.

Therefore a drawing of a bent grid is not the evidence for curvature.

The evidence is the pattern of measured clock rates, trajectories, light paths, timing, and wave signals that agrees with the relativistic equations.

## 5. Geodesic deviation: measurable relative effect

For nearby freely falling worldlines with separation vector $\xi^\mu$ and four-velocity $u^\mu$, relative acceleration is governed by

$$
\frac{D^2\xi^\mu}{D\tau^2}
=
- R^\mu{}_{\nu\alpha\beta}
\,u^\nu\xi^\alpha u^\beta.
$$

This equation is closer to a physical test of curvature than a rubber-sheet analogy because it describes relative motion that can be operationally measured.

## 6. What the popular story gets wrong

### Bad version

A drawn shape in spacetime literally commands matter where to go.

### Better version

A metric field provides a mathematical structure from which proper intervals, free-fall trajectories, redshifts, lensing, and tidal effects are calculated. Experiments test those relationships.

The theory can be interpreted geometrically, and that interpretation is deeply built into general relativity, but the drawing itself is not the mechanism.

## 7. What is measured versus inferred

MEASURED / OPERATIONAL TARGETS include

- clock-rate differences,
- orbital periods and precession,
- light deflection and lensing,
- signal travel times,
- gravitational redshift,
- gravitational-wave detector strain,
- relative acceleration/tidal effects.

INFERRED MATHEMATICAL STRUCTURE includes

- a metric $g_{\mu\nu}$ fitting those relations,
- curvature tensors computed from that metric,
- spacetime geometry as the theory's organizing structure.

INTERPRETIVE LANGUAGE includes statements about whether geometry is the fundamental physical substance, an emergent description, or an effective representation of deeper dynamics.

Those questions should not be confused with whether GR's predictions work.
